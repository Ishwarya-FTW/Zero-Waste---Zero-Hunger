🔬 Scientific Biomethanation Mechanism (CSIR-IICT High-Rate AGR Standard)

When food waste degrades in open dumps, it generates fugitive methane ($CH_4$), a greenhouse gas 28× more potent than $CO_2$. By routing uncollected organic waste to sealed anaerobic digesters, methanogenic bacteria convert volatile fatty acids into enriched biogas (60–65% $CH_4$, 35% $CO_2$). The effluent bio-slurry is separated into nitrogen-rich liquid bio-fertilizer for local farmers and urban gardens.

1. System Architecture & Feasibility (Slide 3 & 4)

Feasibility: Can We Build It?

  • Zero Heavy Hardware at Donor End: Collects only basic operational data: food category, photo, quantity in servings/kg, cooking timestamp, GPS coordinates.
  • API Lightweight: Runs on standard Google Maps Distance Matrix, OpenStreetMap PostGIS routing, and Twilio / Exotel SMS/Call IVR.
  • Pre-existing Infrastructure: NGOs, volunteer transport, and decentralized biomethanizers already exist in major Indian municipal corridors.
  • Rapid College Pilot: Can deploy within 2 weeks across college messes and nearby banquet halls.

Viability: Will It Work Long Term?

  • Zero Raw Material Cost: Food is excess and free; solves donor disposal headache while earning goodwill and CSR credits.
  • Sustainable Revenue Streams: Monetization through CSR partnerships (CGI India model), carbon credits, and tipping fee revenue share from biogas plants.
  • Unique Closed-Loop Model: Solves hunger and landfill waste simultaneously rather than treating them in silos.
  • Govt Alignment: Directly supports Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban 2.0) and national GOBARdhan goals.
